# Evaluating a job ad (B + output format)

**Open when:** the person gives a job link or text, or when ranking ads during a job hunt.

> **In short:** be broad when choosing jobs, never bend facts. The question is not "has this person done
> this before?" but **"can they reasonably do this job?"**

## Rules

| # | Rule |
|---|---|
| B1 | Ask: what are the real daily tasks, which real experience bridges to them, can the gap be learned on the job in reasonable time? |
| B2 | **❌ only for structural blockers:** a language they don't know is truly required (the work happens in it) · a legal licence or mandatory certificate · genuinely senior/manager expertise · a city, shift or contract type they refuse · pay below their minimum (comparing like with like) · legally impossible for their permit ("no sponsorship", "must already have the right to work" while they need sponsorship). |
| B3 | **⚠️ learnable gaps:** ERP/SAP/CRM, a specific program, sector knowledge, company processes, terminology, a different title, basic reporting. Not ❌ unless the ad clearly demands advanced independent experience. |
| B4 | Don't apply year requirements blindly. For junior/specialist roles with "1–2 years", look at the real tasks; similar real experience counts. "5+ years of SAP FI" is real expertise; don't pretend transferable skills cover it. |
| B5 | Fit label, in the person's language: **Strong fit · Good fit · Stretch · No fit.** No percentages (false precision). |
| B6 | **Learnability: Easy · Medium · Hard** + "day one they can… / in 2–8 weeks they learn…". |
| B7 | Every application needs **at least one strong bridge** (real experience → role). No bridge → don't recommend. |
| B8 | "Stretch" is not dropped; present it with reasons: "You can apply; odds low/medium but learnable." **The person decides.** |
| B9 | Ranking: 1 structural fit → 2 bridge → 3 learnability → 4 career value → 5 chance of a reply. |
| B10 | Several applications to one company are fine with a **consistent story**, but at most 2 active per employer in 30 days, both in the same role family; then wait for results (`working-rhythm.md`). |
| B11 | Screening questions ("Do you have X experience?") are answered honestly (No), but the ad isn't dropped for that alone. |
| B12 | **Hard filters of the person:** ads they said "never show me" are not listed, only counted with the reason. Show them if the person asks (wrong filtering gets caught). |
| B13 | **Language:** read the ad (you can read most languages). A language they don't know that is clearly required → ❌. Not clearly required (ad written in the local language but work could be in English, "X is a plus") → ⚠️ "language risk"; unless their hard filter says "never show ads written in X". A **higher level** of a language they do speak (ad wants C1, they have B2) is ⚠️ "level gap" (Stretch), not ❌; say it plainly and write their real level on forms. |
| B14 | **Lesson learned:** for people needing sponsorship or a permit change, big-company application systems (Workday, SuccessFactors…) reject fast. Warn them and suggest other channels: recruiter message, agency, the company's own email. |
| B15 | **Scam signs:** asks for a fee, deposit or "training money" · moves you to Telegram/WhatsApp · "recruiter" writing from a personal Gmail · asks for ID, card or bank details · unrealistic pay ("3000 €/week, no experience") · no company name or website. Warn, don't recommend. |
| B16 | **Compare pay like with like:** gross/net, monthly/yearly/hourly, employment contract vs freelance/B2B, currency. If unclear, don't filter; show with ⚠️. |

## Fit definitions

- **Strong fit:** the core of the job matches real experience; key requirements met; gaps are only
  learnable systems, processes or company knowledge.
- **Good fit:** most of the job can be done with transferable experience; a few new processes or tools;
  1–2 months to adapt is realistic.
- **Stretch:** one core area is clearly new, but no structural blocker. Pay, company, language advantage
  or career value can still make it worth it.
- **No fit:** only for real structural blockers. "Never did this before" alone is not a reason.

**Learnability:** Easy = mostly process, communication, Excel, records, documents, follow-up, coordination,
customer/supplier contact · Medium = new domain knowledge, learnable on the job and by self-study ·
Hard = deep expertise, regulation, licence or years of practice.

## Bridge examples

- supplier work → purchasing, accounts payable, vendor operations
- collections, invoice follow-up → accounts receivable, credit control
- customer relationships → customer support, account management, sales support
- contracts and negotiation → account management, sales
- cash/bank reconciliation → finance operations, payments
- running a small business → back office, operations, document administration
- order and delivery tracking → logistics coordination, order management
- engineering or analytical degree → planning, operations, supply chain
- a language → customer service in that language, teams working with that country

## Output format (same for every ad; in the person's language)

```
**<Role> · <Company> · <City / remote>**  (link)

Fit: Good fit · Learnability: Easy
  Day one: … · In 2–8 weeks: …
Requirements
  ✅ … (met, with which real experience)
  ⚠️ … (learnable or partly met)
  ❌ … (structural blockers only; otherwise "none")
Decision: 1–3 sentences. ("Apply." / "You can apply; medium odds but learnable." / "Don't apply: <blocker>.")
CV plan: 3–5 jobs/lines to lead with, headline idea, bridge: <experience> → <role>
Risks: exaggeration risk, inconsistency, permit/language, scam signs, big-company system (B14)
```

- Keep it short. "Apply" → CV step (`honesty.md` + `SKILL.md` §6).
- "Don't apply" → tracker row with Decision = Skip (not suggested again).
- If you couldn't open the ad, say so; don't evaluate from a guess, ask for the text. If the person pasted
  the text, evaluate from it, say the page itself wasn't verified, and ask them to check the ad is still open.
- Compare the ad's permit wording with the person's recorded answers. If it may conflict: "this may not
  fit your situation; asking the recruiter is cleanest." No legal interpretation.
